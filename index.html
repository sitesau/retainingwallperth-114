Retaining Walls Perth Before You Start Explained

The useful first step is not choosing a finish; it is describing load, water and position. Walk the site and record where the wall sits, the retained height, the length, access for machinery, nearby fences, and whether a driveway, garden bed or structure bears on the ground above. Perth conditions vary from sandy Swan Coastal Plain blocks and limestone backed ground to the different ground found toward the Darling Range hills, so the same material should not be assumed for every block.

Separate the brief into four prompts: what the wall must hold, how water will leave, whether the line is close to a boundary, and whether failure would affect adjoining land. If you want terminology defined before you brief anyone, see more on retaining walls perth glossary.

Match material to site brief

Limestone block suits owners wanting a substantial masonry look that already fits many Perth streetscapes and sandy blocks. Ask how footings are prepared, how moisture is handled behind the wall and how the new finish will meet older paving, garden beds or fence lines.

Post and panel precast concrete, also called twin side retaining, uses posts with concrete panels slotted between them. It is presented as a fast, consistent system for boundary and driveway retaining. Confirm whether excavation, posts, panels and installation are all inside the quoted scope.

  • Timber sleeper: gardens, terraces and lower boundary retaining.
  • Rock or boulder: hills blocks and granite outcropped sites.
  • Besser block: taller retaining, rendered finishes and engineered structural walls.

Treat drainage as structure

Water behind a wall is an engineering issue before it is a landscaping issue. The relevant details are an agricultural drain, gravel and geofabric as part of the drainage and subsoil system, with failure risk linked to winter saturated sandy ground. Before pricing, ask where water is meant to discharge and what happens after heavy rain.

A comparable quote should state the drainage inclusions, not imply them. If one price assumes gravel, geofabric and a drain while another assumes only the wall face, the totals are not like for like. This matters on sloping driveways, boundary lines and garden terraces where trapped water can change footing behaviour over time.

Check approval before design tightens

Approval should be checked early because it can change the paperwork, sequence and cost. A building permit from local government is generally needed once a wall retains more than 0.5 metres of ground. A wall of any height can still require approval if it is connected with other building work, affects or protects adjoining land, or affects a boundary wall or dividing fence.

For engineered retaining, the reference point is AS 4678 and design by a WA registered building engineering practitioner. Ask who prepares footing details and drainage design, who lodges council documents, and whether the fee is for construction only or for the full technical path.

Brief for comparable quotes

Prepare a short written brief before inviting prices: wall location, approximate retained height and length, boundary or dividing fence proximity, what sits above the retained ground, preferred material if known, and visible leaning, cracking, bulging or drainage problems. For an existing wall, state whether the brief is repair, partial rebuild or full replacement after condition has been checked.

Compare quotes by scope rather than total alone. Check that each assumes the same access, removals, retained material, drainage, engineering and permit support. If an engineer, designer or local government has already issued drawings or conditions, those documents should lead the specification; for taller engineered retaining, expect a more formal documentation path from design basis, surcharge, drainage, access and approval responsibility.

  1. Measure and photograph. Record height, length, access, fences, driveways and anything above the retained ground.
  2. Note water behaviour. Look for boggy patches, runoff paths, previous cracking or leaning after winter rain.
  3. Check boundary context. Mark dividing fences, neighbouring fill and any work tied to the wall.
  4. Confirm approval path. Verify permit triggers and whether AS 4678 engineering and council paperwork are likely.
  5. Send a like-for-like brief. Ask each contractor to price drainage, footings, removals, engineering and approvals under the same assumptions.

Common questions

When is a permit likely for a Perth retaining wall? A building permit is generally needed once a wall retains more than 0.5 metres of ground. Approval can still be needed at any height if the wall is tied to other building work, affects or protects adjoining land, or affects a boundary wall or dividing fence.

Which Perth systems are most often compared? The two main systems named for Perth are limestone block and post and panel precast concrete, also described as twin side retaining. Timber, rock, boulder and besser block remain relevant for more specific site briefs.

What should a quote include beyond the wall face? It should state removals, retained material, drainage inclusions and discharge, footing details, whether engineering is required, who handles council steps, and whether damaged work is repair, partial replacement or full replacement.
